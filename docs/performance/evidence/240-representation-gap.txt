=== WHERE THE MISSING INFORMATION ALREADY LIVES ===

Deterministic evidence + document context recovers a large share of the gap:
8 of 19 v1 failures and 2 of 10 v2 failures, with NO model. The information was
already authoritative; it was exposed on the evidence row and the document rather
than on the claim sentence.

The residual splits cleanly into TWO mechanisms, and they need different answers.

--- Mechanism 1: the identifier is in the KEY and PATH but never tokenised ---

  ho-chg-inc-2024-07  'What went wrong in 2024-07 that affected notifier?'
     gold key  incident.2024-07.notifier.root   key overlap: ['notifier']
     path      ops/incidents/2024-07-notifier-duplicate-sms.md
  ho-ev-inc-2024-03  'What rule did the 2024-03 incident put in place?'
     gold key  constraint.orders.pool            key overlap: []
     path      ops/incidents/2024-03-orders-connection-pool-exhaustion.md

The author DID encode the identifier -- in the key and the path. The shipped
tokeniser discards it. This is the defect already found, and it is confined to
questions whose identifier is the only bridge.

--- Mechanism 2: the vocabulary exists NOWHERE in the authoritative record ---

  hv2-temporal_state-04 'Which component handles background messaging in production?'
     gold claim 'The job queue is RabbitMQ.'  Q terms [background, component,
     handle, messag, production]; claim and key contain none of them.
  hv2-why_decision-02  'Why was the old caching layer replaced rather than kept?'
     gold claim 'Redis was chosen for its native TTL and pub/sub support.'
     Q terms [cach, kept, layer, replac] overlap the claim on 'cach' only.

For these the authored text simply does not use the words a user would. No
deterministic re-projection of existing data can close them, because the words
are not anywhere in L0/L1. This is a genuine authoring-vocabulary gap.

=== WHAT THIS RULES OUT ===

- NOT an LLM-extraction problem for most of the gap. Deterministic re-projection
  of data already in the archive closes 10 of 29 blind failures on its own.
- NOT purely a retrieval problem. Mechanism 2 is unreachable by ANY ranking
  because no arm can propose a candidate whose text shares no term with the query.
- An LLM would only address mechanism 2, and would do so by inventing vocabulary
  that is not in the corpus. That is a representation-authoring decision, not a
  retrieval one, and it is out of scope until it is chosen explicitly.
