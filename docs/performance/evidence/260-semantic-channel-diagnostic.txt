--- WHY IT FAILS ---

Evidence-aware embedding makes the semantic channel slightly WORSE, not better.
Gold scores fall more often than they rise on every dataset. The mean margin
between gold and the nearest negative moves NEGATIVE on all three.

The mechanism is dilution. Appending evidence lengthens every candidate, and a
sentence embedding compresses longer inputs toward the mean. All similarities
converge, so separation is lost. The "negatives >= floor" count falls, which is
not a safety gain: those negatives no longer clear the floor on similarity alone
because the whole scale shifted, not because they became less relevant.

--- THE STRUCTURAL FINDING (more important than the number) ---

The requested design assumed the lexical gate is a separate acceptance stage that
could be left untouched. Reading the code, it is not:

  api/services/memory_relevance.py:274-282   score(): lexical -> token overlap,
                                             else   -> cosine(topic, claim)
  api/services/memory_relevance.py:301-303   gate E:
        accepted = value >= 0.30 and (
            overlap or value >= 0.65 or isclose(value, 0.30))

In PRODUCTION (lexical=False) `value` IS the semantic cosine score, and it is the
acceptance threshold. `overlap` is lexical token overlap, computed from the SAME
`representations` list at line 230-231.

So the representation is not one channel with two consumers. It is the input to
BOTH:
  - the cosine that decides acceptance (through `value`)
  - the lexical overlap that also decides acceptance (through `overlap`)

There is no way to enrich the embedding while holding the acceptance boundary
fixed, because the acceptance boundary IS the embedding. That is precisely why
the previous contextual-L2 change moved 63 packs and why the fix cannot be
applied by separating two functions: they were never separate.

This also explains the earlier result more precisely. The 63-pack regression was
not only `overlap` widening. It was the cosine floor itself moving.

--- DECISION ---

REJECT. Do not implement the production change.

Case B from the milestone's own classification: semantic scores do not improve,
and no true candidates become newly reachable. Nothing here justifies touching
production retrieval.

The correct next question is NOT a representation change. The offline study
promised 10/29 recoveries from adding evidence text; the embedding channel cannot
deliver them, because adding text dilutes a sentence embedding rather than
sharpening it. That offline number was measured on LEXICAL token overlap, where
extra terms genuinely add signal. It does not transfer to a bi-encoder.
